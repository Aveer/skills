# Surfaces and geometry

Optional observations adapted from the upstream MIT-licensed skill, not a
replacement for project tokens, shell composition or shared control ownership.

- For nested rounded surfaces intended to look concentric, inner radius plus
  separation can suggest an outer radius. Joined panels, square boundaries and
  intentional offsets are equally valid designs.
- Optical alignment can improve asymmetric icons; inspect the actual icon and
  surrounding controls before adjusting geometry.
- Borders, dividers, outlines, shadows and flat surfaces are different ways to
  communicate boundaries or elevation. Choose according to the established
  semantic roles and contrast, not a general rule favoring shadows or borders.
- Images sometimes benefit from a subtle separator on a similar background.
  The existing theme, palette, image presentation and contrast own that choice.
  An outline is not mandatory; no particular color or opacity is prescribed.
- Distinguish visible geometry from interactive target size. Apply the relevant
  accessibility requirements and product input modes without sacrificing
  necessary expert density or allowing overlapping hit targets. An expanded
  hit area is one option when its behavior is correct.
- Compare related states and consumers when changing a shared primitive. Do
  not turn a one-off observation into a new global visual rule.
